# Error analysis on MELD test (§10.8)

The deployed system on 2610 test utterances. The branches disagree (different labels, both calibrated confidences ≥ 0.4) on **352** clips (13.5%). On those clips the sound branch is right 37.5% of the time, the text branch 38.6%, and the fused answer 44.0%. On the other clips the fused answer is right 58.9% of the time.

## Discussion

This section is written by hand. The tables above are regenerated by `python -m emotion_analyzer.evaluate report`, which keeps this section.

- **Neutral absorbs every class.** Neutral is 48% of MELD test (1,256 of 2,610) and 47% of train (4,703 of 9,976). For every other class, neutral is the most common wrong answer: sadness 54%, fear 50%, joy 46%, disgust 43%, anger 36%, surprise 28%. Neutral recall is 79%. Most MELD utterances are short and conversational, and the annotators labelled them while watching the video in context. A single utterance heard alone often carries little emotion in either the voice or the words.
- **Disgust and fear are almost never found.** Fused recall is 8.8% for disgust and 10.0% for fear. The two classes have only 271 and 268 training clips (under 3% each), even with class-weighted loss. The text branch names disgust only twice in 2,610 clips: both times correctly, but that gives 2.9% recall. In the fused output, disgust is mostly taken for neutral (43%) or anger (26%).
- **The branches are good at different classes.** The text branch finds sadness far better than the sound branch (recall 32.2% against 14.4%): in a sitcom, sad lines are often delivered lightly, so the words carry more of the sadness than the voice does. The sound branch is stronger on anger (36.8% against 31.9%) and joy (38.6% against 35.8%), where loudness and laughter help. Surprise is found equally by both, and fusing them lifts it to 53.0%.
- **Why the fused answer leans on sound.** The weight fitted on dev is w_sound 0.75. It maximises weighted-F1 on all of dev, but it costs sadness recall (fused 19.2% against text 32.2%). The same happens to joy in the "We did have fun, didn't we?" clip: the words were right, and the voice outweighed them. A per-class weight could recover some of this, but it would be fitted on only 111 dev sadness clips, so it is left as future work.
- **Disagreements are informative.** The branches disagree, both with at least 0.4 confidence, on 13.5% of test clips. There the fused answer is right 44% of the time, more than either branch alone (37.5% and 38.6%), but well below its 58.9% on the other clips. That is why the app shows the disagreement banner instead of hiding it.
- **Some errors are label ambiguity or subtitle problems, not model errors.**
  - "I have no idea what you just said." is labelled neutral, heard as anger and read as surprise. Without the scene, any of the three is defensible.
  - MELD's subtitles are sometimes cut short ("Would" for "Would you care to venture one?", "Oh." for "Oh. Oh, right."). That is also why Whisper's insertions inflate the WER (`wer.md`).
  - These clips put a ceiling on what any utterance-level system can score on MELD.
