# Error analysis on MELD test (§10.8)

The deployed system on 2610 test utterances. The branches disagree (different labels, both calibrated confidences ≥ 0.4) on **352** clips (13.5%). On those clips the sound branch is right 37.5% of the time, the text branch 38.6%, and the fused answer 45.7%. On the other clips the fused answer is right 59.9% of the time.

## Discussion

This section is written by hand. The tables above are regenerated by `python -m emotion_analyzer.evaluate report`, which keeps this section. The figures are for the deployed system after the v1.4 refit (w_sound 0.50).

- **Neutral absorbs every class.** Neutral is 48% of MELD test (1,256 of 2,610) and 47% of train (4,703 of 9,976).
  - For every other class, neutral is the most common wrong answer: fear 48%, sadness 47%, joy 46%, anger 41%, disgust 41%, surprise 31%. Neutral recall is 80.5%.
  - Most MELD utterances are short and conversational, and the annotators labelled them while watching the video in context. A single utterance heard alone often carries little emotion in either the voice or the words.
- **Disgust and fear are almost never found.** Fused recall is 5.9% for disgust and 12.0% for fear.
  - The two classes have only 271 and 268 training clips (under 3% each), even with class-weighted loss.
  - The text branch names disgust only twice in 2,610 clips. Both times it is right, but that gives 2.9% recall.
  - In the fused output, disgust is mostly taken for neutral (41%) or anger (29%).
- **The branches are good at different classes.**
  - The text branch finds sadness far better than the sound branch (recall 32.2% against 14.4%). In a sitcom, sad lines are often delivered lightly, so the words carry more of the sadness than the voice does, as in "I guess I was so worried about having to come here and do that."
  - The sound branch is stronger on anger (36.8% against 31.9%) and joy (38.6% against 35.8%), where loudness and laughter help, as in the shouted "To put the camera away!", which the words alone read as neutral.
  - Surprise is found equally by both, and fusing them lifts it to 50.5%.
- **What the refit changed.**
  - The weight fitted on all of dev was w_sound 0.75. It suited the six main characters (86% of dev) and let the voice outvote the words: fused sadness recall was 19.2%.
  - With w_sound 0.50, chosen on the dev speakers outside the main cast (§8.4, v1.4), fused sadness recall is 31.2%, close to the text branch. Anger stays close to the sound branch (36.2% against 36.8%), and weighted-F1 rises from 54.5 to 55.7.
  - A per-class weight could gain more, but it would be fitted on very few dev clips per class, so it is left as future work.
- **Disagreements are informative.** The branches disagree, both with at least 0.4 confidence, on 13.5% of test clips.
  - There the fused answer is right 45.7% of the time, more than either branch alone (37.5% and 38.6%), but well below its 59.9% on the other clips.
  - That is why the app shows the disagreement banner instead of hiding it.
- **Some errors are label ambiguity or subtitle problems, not model errors.**
  - "I have no idea what you just said." is labelled neutral, heard as anger and read as surprise. Without the scene, any of the three is defensible.
  - MELD's subtitles and audio clips don't always line up:
    - Whisper heard "She'd be happy to see me." where the subtitle is "Here's Frannie. Hmm, won't she be happy to see me?"
    - Whisper heard more than the subtitle in "Are you sure you're okay? Oh, yeah, yeah. Don't worry about me. I'll be fine."
    - That is also why Whisper's insertions inflate the WER (`wer.md`).
  - These clips put a ceiling on what any utterance-level system can score on MELD.
